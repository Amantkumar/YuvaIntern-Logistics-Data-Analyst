This report presents the work completed for Week 3 of the Yuva Intern Logistics Data Analyst Internship, focusing on Advanced Data Analysis and Visualization in Logistics. The purpose of this task was to analyze a cleaned logistics dataset, calculate meaningful performance metrics, identify patterns and relationships, and communicate the results through clear Python visualizations.

The analysis continues from the data preparation work completed in Week 2. The cleaned dataset contains shipment-level information such as delivery date, region, vehicle type, traffic level, distance, shipment volume, promised delivery time, actual delivery time, transportation cost, on-time status, and delay status. The dataset is illustrative and simulated for internship purposes.

The exploratory data analysis begins with dataset inspection and descriptive statistics to understand central tendency, spread, minimum and maximum values, and the overall distribution of important numerical variables. Key logistics KPIs such as on-time delivery rate, delay rate, average delivery time, average transportation cost, average distance, and average shipment volume are also calculated.

Seven visualizations are included in the report. These include a delivery-time distribution, delivery time by vehicle type, on-time delivery by region, distance versus delivery time, distance versus transportation cost, a numerical correlation heatmap, and a monthly delivery-time trend. Each visualization is selected according to the analytical question it addresses. Histograms help understand distributions, box plots support group comparison, bar charts compare regional KPIs, scatter plots examine relationships, heatmaps summarize correlations, and line charts show time-based trends.

The report also provides regional and vehicle-level summaries and discusses practical insights and recommendations. The analysis is descriptive and does not assume causation from correlation. The Python implementation uses pandas for data manipulation and matplotlib for visualization.

The completed outputs provide an analytical foundation for Week 4, where predictive modeling and optimization techniques can be applied to the logistics problem.
